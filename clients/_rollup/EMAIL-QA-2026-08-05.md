# Email Copy QA — 43 campaigns, 11 clients, against the 5-point checklist

**Date:** 2026-08-05 · **Source:** Evergreen only (`/api/clients/{slug}/report` + `/stats`) · **Corpus:** [output/email-qa-corpus.json](output/email-qa-corpus.json) · **Puller:** [tools/email_qa_pull.py](../../tools/email_qa_pull.py)

The checklist under test:

1. Ensure your message is 100% confusion-free
2. Remove all overused AI language
3. Every line must flow to the next — create cohesion
4. Be radically disarming — use self-deprecation
5. Don't sound like everyone else — use pattern disruptors

**Grading is my own read of these five, not the repo's rubrics.** Almost all existing QA material here (Voice Profile, `sms_qa.py`, the 13-item qa-checklist) is SMS-tuned, and scoring email against SMS rules wouldn't test the checklist — it would test the SMS playbook. My definitions are in §2 and are the first thing worth redlining.

**Limitations — read these before trusting any number below:**

- **No subject lines.** The reconstruction is body-only. Criteria 1, 2 and 5 are graded on body copy alone. Subject lines are un-QA'd and remain the biggest blind spot.
- **Survivorship bias in the corpus itself.** Copy is only recoverable where at least one person replied. Campaigns that got total silence have no copy and are invisible here. This corpus is skewed toward copy that worked *at least once*.
- **seedx contributed nothing.** Its 7 copies exist in the report but match no EmailBison campaign in Airtable — a data gap in the sync, not an empty client. Worth chasing separately.
- **9 of 43 aren't cold opens — and this is a reconstruction bug, not missing data.** Evergreen returned mid-thread replies and sequence steps as if they were first touches. I tagged these `MID`/`SEQ` and excluded them from the cold-open stats. **Cause, confirmed against `/api/clients/redo/deals`:** in a captured thread, the cold open sits inside the *quoted tail* of the prospect's inbound reply (after `… wrote:`), while the only block tagged `Outbound - … said:` is the rep's manual follow-up. The reconstruction keys off that `Outbound` marker, so it can return the follow-up and discard the opener. The same parser explains the thread contamination in [3], [35] and [36], where an `Outbound - … said:` block is stapled onto the end of an otherwise-correct opener.

  **Worked example — redo [29].** Evergreen returned a scheduling message ("which timezone you in?"). The actual running copy, confirmed by Aaman and visible in the quoted tail of the same threads, is: *"Hey, someone just abandoned their cart on {store}'s site without opting into your marketing flows - how do you pull them back to complete the transaction? … Not asking for your business, but could I at least demo how it works?"* That copy scores 5/5 — including the cleanest criterion-4 pass in the entire corpus — against the 2/5 recorded for what was reconstructed. **Treat every `MID`/`SEQ` row as a floor on that client's copy quality, not a measurement of it.**
- **Thread contamination.** Four records ([3], [35], [36], and [4]) have later reply-thread messages appended to the body; [17] carries a Proofpoint spam-scan footer. I graded only the outbound cold-email portion. The Proofpoint footer is itself a finding — that campaign is passing through a spam appliance.
- **Positives are rare events.** Most campaigns have 0–4 positives on thousands of sends. Every performance comparison below is directional, not significant.

## 2. How I graded each criterion

**1. Confusion-free.** One pass, cold reader: who is this, what do they do, why me, why now, what's the ask? Fails on a sentence that needs re-reading, an unexplained mechanism, a pronoun or merge variable with no clear referent, or two competing asks.

**2. Overused AI language.** Not a word list. The tell is copy that could have been generated without knowing anything about the sender or the recipient — empty verbs, abstractions standing in for what actually happened, triadic lists, stacked adjectives, superlatives with no number. Industry-native terminology is *not* slop. I also failed this criterion for **spintax degradation** — copy mangled by synonym-spinning ("I assisted Vital Psych MD create their own patient flow") reads machine-made for a different reason, but reads machine-made.

**3. Flow / cohesion.** Read aloud: does each sentence pick up something from the one before, or do they sit side by side? Where a follow-up exists, it has to *continue* the first email, not restate it.

**4. Radically disarming / self-deprecation.** I set a strict bar, because a loose one passes almost everything. A pass needs one of: (a) self-deprecation — admitting a flaw or the awkwardness of your own outreach; (b) a genuine out that costs you the sale ("all good on my end", "already handled?"); (c) conceding your own lead offer might not fit. **Not** a pass: a hedge that introduces a brag ("this might sound bold, but…"), a soft CTA, a 30-day pilot or guarantee (that's commercial risk-reversal, not tone), or the compliance opt-out footer — every email here has one.

**5. Pattern disruptors.** Competitor-swap test: could a rival paste their logo on this and send it unchanged? If yes, fail. Does anything in the first two lines break the shape the reader has already deleted five times this morning?

## 4. Criterion by criterion — what's actually failing

### 1. Confusion-free — 60%, and the failures are mostly defects, not writing

This is the worst-performing criterion after disarming, and almost none of the failures are *thinking* problems. They're broken text shipped to thousands of people:

- **Visible merge-variable bugs.** [41] wise_digital opens `"Hey {first_name}, Checked out and saw you work with nonprofit organizations"` — "Checked out" *what*? — and later `"Put together a couple ideas for ."` with an empty variable and an orphan full stop. **This went to 4,134 people and is the joint-best-performing campaign in the corpus.** [42] has `"a few growth ideas for Han"`. [14] go_fish greets the company instead of the person: `"Hi {company}, Really appreciate you being open to it."`
- **Spintax damage.** [23] leadgenix, the largest send in the corpus at 23,614: `"we helped Vital Psych MD that outcome"` — the verb is missing. [26]: `"I assisted Vital Psych MD in Miami create their own patient flow."` [24]: `"They these days see 7-9 inbound sessions a week."`
- **Duplicated words.** [38] and [40] scaletopia both ship `"they're at capacity for client capacity."`
- **Sender/calendar mismatch.** [29] redo — Redo's only currently-sending campaign — is signed `Taylor Mitchell` but claims availability against `calendly.com/henry-redo`: *"I'm fairly open mornings MST, or feel free to find a time on our calendar here:https://calendly.com/henry-redo/redo-recover"*. The person claiming the mornings isn't the person whose calendar it is, and the URL is jammed against the colon with no space, which some clients render as one broken token.
- **[27] leadgenix is the worst single line in the corpus:** `"Hey, No I idea how this slipped in my inbox - lmk if it still sounds interested."` Word order broken twice, and the premise is incoherent — why is he telling the prospect something slipped into *his own* inbox? Its T1 and T2 are also byte-identical, so the follow-up sends the same email twice.

The cleanest work on this criterion is [10] digital_resource and [16] go_fish — both short, both specific, neither with a stray character.

### 2. Overused AI language — 72%

Genuinely better than expected. Most of this copy is concrete: named brands, real numbers, industry-native terms (LSAs, map pack, Psychology Today, contested probate billables). The failures cluster in two shapes:

- **The triadic mechanism.** Chamber's `"a blend of humor, education, and entertainment"` appears in [3], [6] and [8], always attached to `"a specific way to run performance creative"` — a mechanism that's asserted and never described. [2] is the worst: `"leaning into the contrast vs typical collagen messaging in a more visual / slightly unexpected way"` could mean anything.
- **The numbered 3-point list.** [35]/[36] scaletopia: three bullets, then a stranded fragment `"running text outreach so we can directly reach the decision makers"` with no lead-in. That bulleted shape is the single most recognisable AI-email silhouette in the corpus. It also ships `"your 229.85% ROI"` — a two-decimal figure lifted straight off their website, which reads scraped rather than researched.
- **Spintax damage** (counted here per §2): "obtain comparable outcomes", "I assisted… create", "They these days see".

### 3. Flow / cohesion — 76%, the strongest criterion

Most of this copy does chain properly. The best example in 43 campaigns is [4] chamber_media, where every sentence is caused by the one before it:

> "small confession: I've been studying instant coffee niche ads for a few weeks and I keep mixing up which brand is which. Not because I'm bad at my job (I hope). Because every brand in your category is running the same UGC, the same hooks, the same percentage-off badges. Purity Coffee and most of their competitors are basically interchangeable in the feed — which is the actual reason CPAs keep climbing."

Confusion → why he's confused → why that's true → why that costs them money. Nothing there could be reordered.

[20] kynship is the other one: `"I know you're constantly pitched"` → `"But we recently helped scale Purdy & Figg"` → `"Figured I'd shoot this over since"` → `"Not sure if it's relevant currently, but mind if I share"`. Every clause hinges on the previous.

The failures are of one type: **follow-ups that restate instead of continue.** [19] growth_lab's T2 is a near-verbatim rewrite of T1 with synonyms swapped. [9] digital_resource's T2 re-explains streaming TV from scratch as if T1 never happened. [27] sends the identical text twice. A follow-up that repeats the first email tells the reader the first email was the whole pitch.

### 4. Radically disarming — 37%, and it's the real gap

Two-thirds of the corpus has nothing that lowers the reader's guard. **The dominant substitute is a hedge that introduces a brag**, which I counted as a fail:

- [0] `"you might not know me but I was head of performance marketing at Meta for 5 years"`
- [11]/[13] `"this might sound bold, but I think TikTok Shop could be one of {company}'s biggest revenue channels"`
- [42] `"Know this is out of the ordinary but we're the marketing partners that took Dark Horse CPA from $1.5m to $20m"`
- [31]/[34]/[37] `"I'd guess you see a lot of these emails, but…"`

These sound disarming and aren't. The structure is *acknowledge the reader's skepticism, then immediately confirm it*. Nothing is conceded and nothing is risked.

**Actual self-deprecation appears exactly once in 43 campaigns** — [4] chamber_media: `"Not because I'm bad at my job (I hope)"`, and its closing `"if your ads are already working - just reply no. I'll take the L and go finally book that honeymoon I keep putting away."` That's the only place a sender makes himself smaller.

The other genuine passes are the "costs you the sale" out rather than self-deprecation: [1] `"all good on my end"`, [23] `"Feel free to ignore if you've got a packed caseload"`, [30] `"or do you already have this dialled in?"`, [7] `"if humor doesn't fit {company}'s brand, that's totally fine"`.

By this checklist's standard, criterion 4 is where our email copy is weakest by a distance. **Whether that's a problem is a separate question — see §6.**

### 5. Pattern disruptors — 81% of cold opens, but concentrated in research, not voice

The passes are almost entirely *specificity that required work*, not clever writing:

- [10] digital_resource names the prospect's two actual local competitors: `"noticed Air doctors & Norris mechanical are the top searches, getting most of the calls."`
- [41] wise_digital: `"For most firms your size, referrals carry things through April then it gets quiet."` Only someone who knows CPA seasonality writes that.
- [16] go_fish offers a scorecard benchmarking them `"vs Sideshow and Good Smile Company"` — named competitors, unswappable.
- [8] chamber: `"Hardly any NetworkTech SaaS have — most are still sitting on LinkedIn."`
- [42] wise_digital: `"ENGAGE opens next week & I'll be at #705"` — a booth number and a date.
- [33] scaletopia opens with a diagnosis instead of a claim: `"are you not running cold outreach because you've been burnt or you just don't believe in it?"`

The failures are the templates: growth_lab's three near-identical legal-SEO emails ([17], [18], [19]) with the `"We just take on 3 firms per city"` scarcity line, and scaletopia's bulleted-list variants ([35], [36], [39]).

**One caveat that cuts against several passes:** `"I know you're constantly pitched by agencies"` (kynship), `"I'm sure your inbox is packed with agencies claiming…"` (go_fish), `"I know this looks like just another lead gen pitch"` (scaletopia), `"I'd guess you see a lot of these emails"` (scaletopia ×3) — six clients independently arrived at the same opener. Inside our own corpus, *acknowledging the crowded inbox is now the crowded-inbox move.* It reads as a disruptor only to someone who hasn't seen the other five.

## 5. Cross-client patterns

**Copy quality tracks how much research went in, not how well it's written.** Every high scorer is carrying a fact that took work to obtain — a competitor name, a seasonal cycle, a booth number, a directory the prospect is listed on. Every low scorer is carrying a claim that could be sent to anyone in the vertical.

**Spintax is actively damaging leadgenix and scaletopia.** The synonym-spinning that generates variants is producing broken English at scale — the 23,614-send campaign has a missing verb in its proof sentence. This is not a copywriting problem and won't be fixed by rewriting; it's the spinner.

**Scaletopia quotes the Chamber Media case study with different numbers in every campaign:** 12 retainers ([33] T1), 14 retainers / 74K MRR ([31], [34] T2), 14 retainers / 72K MRR ([33] T2), 17 retainers ([35], [36]), 84 proposal meetings ([30] T2), and "84 agencies" vs "32 agencies" vs "26 agencies" across [31], [32], [33]. Wise Digital does the same with Dark Horse: `$1.5M → $21M across 50+ locations` in [41], `$1.5m/yr → $20m/yr in 5 yrs` in [42]. Any prospect who gets two of these — or checks — sees a number that moves.

**The follow-up is the weakest asset across every client.** Where T2 exists it usually restates T1. Nobody is using the second email to say something the first one couldn't.

**Chamber Media has the best and among the worst copy in the corpus**, from the same sender in the same month: [4] at 5/5 and [2] at 1/5, both Ian Johnson. The 5/5 is a written email; the 1/5 is a pasted list of links.

## 6. Where the checklist disagrees with our performance data

Cold opens only, sent ≥ 500 (n=29). Rates are per 1,000 sends. **Treat every line as directional** — positives are rare events, the campaigns differ by client, niche, offer and list, and nothing here is controlled.

| criterion | passed | booked/1k | power/1k | failed | booked/1k | power/1k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. confusion-free | n=20 | 0.19 | 0.25 | n=9 | 0.17 | 0.25 |
| 2. no AI language | n=22 | **0.21** | **0.30** | n=7 | 0.05 | 0.05 |
| 3. flow / cohesion | n=23 | **0.21** | **0.27** | n=6 | **0.00** | 0.15 |
| 4. **disarming** | n=11 | **0.13** | **0.21** | n=18 | **0.23** | **0.29** |
| 5. pattern disruptor | n=24 | **0.20** | **0.27** | n=5 | **0.00** | **0.00** |

**Criteria 2, 3 and 5 hold up.** Every campaign that failed flow or failed the disruptor test booked nothing at all. Failing "no AI language" cut booking rate by 4×. If you only enforce three items on this list, enforce those.

**Criterion 1 does nothing.** Confusion-free campaigns and confused ones book at the same rate (0.19 vs 0.17). The reason is visible in the corpus: the failures are typos and merge-variable bugs, not incoherent arguments — and [41], the joint-best campaign in the corpus, ships two visible merge bugs and still books 6 meetings on 4,134 sends. Prospects appear to forgive broken text when the insight underneath is right. That is not an argument for shipping broken text; it's an argument that this criterion isn't where the leverage is.

**Criterion 4 runs the wrong way.** Campaigns I graded as disarming booked at 0.13/1k; campaigns with no disarming at all booked at 0.23/1k — nearly double. Power requests point the same direction (0.21 vs 0.29).

I don't think that means disarming hurts. The likelier explanation is in the corpus: the copy that passes criterion 4 tends to be *softer overall* — permission-asking, low-commitment, "mind if I share" — while the strongest performers ([41] wise_digital, 6 booked; [42], 5 booked on 62 sends) lead with a hard specific insight and no softening at all. Criterion 4 is probably picking up a tone that correlates with weaker asks rather than causing anything. But on this data it is **the one item on the list with no evidence behind it**, and it's also the item we fail most often. Failing it does not look like it's costing us meetings.

**The two above-client-average campaigns split on the checklist.** [20] kynship scores 5/5 and books 3 on 1,834. [41] wise_digital scores 3/5 — failing confusion-free and disarming — and books 6 on 4,134. What they share isn't checklist compliance; it's that both know something true and specific about the reader's situation (`"we're crushing it in the UK scene rn"` with two named UK brands; `"referrals carry things through April then it gets quiet"`).

**Score-to-outcome is not monotonic.** 5/5 campaigns book at 0.39/1k, 3/5 at 0.25, 4/5 at 0.14, 2/5 and 1/5 at zero. The 4/5 dip is small-n noise (13 campaigns, 7 bookings), but it's a fair warning against reading the total score as a quality ranking. The floor is real — nothing below 3/5 booked anything — the ceiling is not.

## 7. What I'd fix, in order

1. **Kill the spintax on leadgenix and scaletopia**, or gate it behind a grammar check. Broken proof sentences are going out at 23k/campaign. This is the cheapest fix on the list and it isn't a copy fix.
2. **Add a merge-variable and empty-string check before launch.** `"Checked out and saw you work with…"` and `"Put together a couple ideas for ."` shipped to 4,134 people on our best-performing campaign.
3. **Fix the case-study numbers.** Pick one figure for Chamber Media and one for Dark Horse and use it everywhere. Right now the same proof carries five different numbers.
4. **Rewrite the follow-ups.** Most T2s restate T1. [27] literally sends the same email twice. The second email should carry the objection the first one raised.
5. **Retire the "I know your inbox is full of agencies" opener.** Six clients are running it simultaneously. It stopped being a pattern interrupt.
6. **Treat criterion 4 as an open question, not a fix.** We fail it 63% of the time and the data doesn't say that's costing us. If you want to test it, the clean experiment is [41] wise_digital — best performer, no disarming at all — split against a version with a genuine out added. That's a real A/B, not a rewrite.
