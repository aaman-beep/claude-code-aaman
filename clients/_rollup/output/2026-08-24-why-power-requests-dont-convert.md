## The short version

There is one finding and everything else is a consequence of it.

**We answer every positive reply with the same sentence.**

> "Awesome. If you have got a few minutes [time] or maybe next week, I'd be happy to hop on
> a quick call and discuss what we can do for [Company]."

- **17 of the 30** replies we answered got that sentence near-verbatim.
- **21 of 30** contain "if you have got a few minutes."
- **17 of 30** contain "hop on a quick call."
- **8** end on "what we can do for [Company]."

It goes out unchanged to "Yes", to "Sure give me a call", to "Show me", to "we have two
firms trying to sell us and we will be paying the best fit", and to a man who had already
written "Take me off your list!!!"

**After that sentence, 15 of 17 never wrote again.**

That is the machine. It isn't a list problem and it isn't a speed problem — it's that the
warmest moment in the entire funnel is handled by a mail-merge.

## Three things that are NOT the problem

Worth killing these first so nobody spends a week on them.

**Speed.** Median time from their reply to ours is **19 minutes**. 18 of 30 are inside half
an hour. We are fast and it buys us nothing. Your instinct that the 20-minute reply time is
beside the point is right — don't build an SLA, fix the sentence.

**Follow-up volume.** Kevin Atkinson got the same nudge twice, word for word, and still
went quiet. More of the same message is not more.

**Lead quality, mostly.** Growth Lab's list gave us a Managing Partner, two Partners, a
President and a VP who all said yes. The list did its job. We lost them after.

## What's actually breaking, in order of how much it costs

### 1. The stock line is not an ask — it's an if-clause

"**If** you have got a few minutes X **or maybe** next week, I'd be happy to..."

There is no question in it. Silence is a valid answer to it. And "or maybe next week"
reopens the door the first half just closed, so even the one concrete time stops being a
decision. Then it closes on "discuss what we can do for [Company]" — a sentence about us.

Your note on Erica Swerdlow — *"you're not really asking directly"* — is the whole thing.
She wrote **"Yes let's discuss."** We wrote back a conditional. She's still open.

Same on Kate Bell: **"Sounds perfect 😍"** → the stock line → silence.

### 5. The opener manufactures shrugs, and we then treat them as bookings

**15 of 35 replies are three words or fewer.** "Sure." "Yes." "Ok." "Show me."

That's not apathy — it's the exact answer our own CTA asks for:

> "Could I show you how it works?" · "Lmk if it's worth exploring this week?" ·
> "Any chance you're free this week?"

These are permission questions. "Sure" means *you may show me*. It does not mean *book me*.
So a low-commitment yes enters the CRM tagged **Power Request**, and then gets answered as
if it were booking-ready. Two mismatches stacked on top of each other.

This is the one that needs a copy decision, not a process fix: **if the opener asked for
permission, the next message has to earn the meeting — it can't assume it.**

## What I'd change — strawman, argue with it

**1. Kill the stock line.** It has 15 silences against 17 sends. Nothing that follows
matters until this is gone.

**2. Replace the ask with a decision, not a condition.** One time, no escape hatch, ends on
a question mark:

> "Nice one — Thursday 2pm your time? I'll send the invite now."

No "if". No "or maybe next week". No "what we can do for [Company]". If they can't do
Thursday they'll say so, and that's a reply — which is the whole point.

**3. Never ask for the email in order to book.** If we have their number we can hold the
time and ask for the address *after* they've agreed to it — or send the link. And when a
reply already contains a booking link or a phone number, use it. Barben and Ripp both
handed us the mechanism and got the mail-merge back.

**4. Answer the question they asked before pivoting to a call.** "Send it over" means send
it. "Share some videos" means more than one, with a line on why it's relevant to them. The
call ask goes *after* the thing they asked for, in the same message.

**5. Split "Power Request" into two tags at the source.** *Asked for a call* vs *agreed to
be shown something*. They need different second messages, and merging them is what lets the
mail-merge look reasonable. It also fixes the denominator.

**6. Fix the suppression list before anything else.** Terry Brumley asked off and got six
more messages from three names. That's the one item here with a cost beyond conversion.

## Open questions for you

- **Is the opener's permission CTA doing more harm than good?** It gets replies, but it
  gets *"Sure"* — and "Sure" is where this whole thing dies. Worth testing an opener that
  asks for the time directly, even if raw reply rate drops.
- **Who owns the reply?** Every failure above is one person, one message, ten seconds of
  judgement. Whether that's a human or a better-scripted bot changes the fix entirely.
- **Chamber ecom vs SaaS** — you said only Hydrate Heroes, Seattle Sutton and LactiGo are
  the real ecom ones. If SaaS PRs consistently need a different second message, that's a
  segment split, not a copy tweak.
