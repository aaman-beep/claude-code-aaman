## The Angle

John gave us the segmentation logic himself, unprompted:

> *"One of the reasons a dispensary and home services work so well and the banking world is because the organic Google and AI searches are really valuable to them. When someone's like 'bank near me' or 'plumber near me' it's still massively on Google searches — like the AI is now too. It's how people find that service. CPAs are different — usually it's a referral."*

So the qualifying test for every segment is: **is search the primary way this buyer's customers find them?** If yes, organic is a revenue channel and NextLeft's proof lands. If it's referral-led (CPAs, wealth), organic is a nice-to-have and we're pushing uphill.

The second half of the angle is a proof problem, not a targeting problem. NextLeft publishes in **keywords and monthly traffic value** — "95,900 keywords, $613,000/mo traffic value." That's a metric an SEO buyer recognises and a cold prospect does not feel. But the revenue numbers *do* exist, they're just buried in client quotes:

- **Fiskars: revenue increased 300%**
- **ShipCalm: 1,500%+ increase in monthly SQLs**
- **Midigator: 587% monthly increase in SEO leads**

**Every campaign leads with those three and nothing else.** Keyword counts move to the second line as scale proof, never the hook.

Third piece: John's own objection to eCommerce is the angle for eCommerce. He said *"e-commerce is great, but it's so competitive — you're competing against Amazon, plus all these other shops."* That is precisely what a mid-market brand feels every day, and Fiskars, Gilmour, MonkeySports, Paper Mart and Reima are all brands that sell through retail *and* their own store and won that fight anyway. We don't dodge the competitiveness — we name it and point at Fiskars.

## ICP Segment 1
**Mid-Market eCommerce & DTC Retail ($10M–$75M)**

Consumer brands that sell through retail partners *and* their own store, watching Amazon and their own retail channel outrank them for their own products and categories. They have an in-house marketing lead who is measured on organic but has no SEO specialist, and every agency they've talked to pitched percentage lifts instead of revenue. We lead with Fiskars — a household name in their exact position — and the only hard revenue number NextLeft owns.

**Decision Makers**

- Director of eCommerce / Head of eCommerce
- Senior Digital Marketing Manager / Director of Digital Marketing
- VP Marketing / CMO
- *(Titles mirrored from NextLeft's actual client quotes — Fiskars, Paper Mart and MonkeySports testimonials all come from exactly these roles)*

**Signal Points**

- Shopify Plus, Magento or BigCommerce storefront confirmed via BuiltWith / Storeleads
- Product stocked at a national retailer (Target, Walmart, Home Depot, Dick's, REI, Ace) **and** running their own DTC store — the retail + owned split is the qualifier
- Amazon or a retail partner currently outranking the brand for its own category head term
- Organic keyword count flat or declining YoY against a named direct competitor
- Replatform or site migration in the last 12 months, or posted / recently removed an in-house SEO or eCommerce role

**Lead Case Studies**

- **Fiskars — revenue increased 300%**, 77,800 ranked keywords, $108,000/mo organic traffic value *(quoted by their Senior Digital Marketing Manager)*
- **Paper Mart** — 95,900 ranked keywords, $613,000/mo traffic value; their Director of e-Commerce cites *"remarkable upward trends in keyword rankings, traffic, and revenue"*
- **Gilmour** — $137,000/mo traffic value, 5x referring domains — *"NextLeft has probably given us the greatest ROI our team has seen"*
- **Hallmark, MonkeySports, Reima** — namedrop stack for credibility weight

## ICP Segment 2
**B2B Services, Logistics & SaaS ($10M–$100M)**

B2B companies whose buyers research on Google — and increasingly in AI answers — before they ever take a call. They're usually paying Google Ads for terms they should own organically, publishing content that doesn't rank, and have no idea whether they're being cited in ChatGPT or AI Overviews. We lead with ShipCalm because it's the only NextLeft case study measured in pipeline rather than traffic, and it's quoted by a CEO, which lets us go straight at founder-led B2B.

**Decision Makers**

- VP Marketing / Head of Demand Generation / Director of Marketing
- CMO at the upper end of the range
- CEO / Co-Founder at founder-led B2B under ~$25M *(ShipCalm's testimonial is from the CEO & Co-Founder — the proof is pre-tuned to that seat)*

**Signal Points**

- Running paid search on their own category terms while ranking off page 1 organically — paying for traffic they should own
- Active blog or resource hub publishing consistently with no corresponding keyword growth
- Recently raised, or PE / VC-backed with a growth mandate
- Hired or posted for a demand gen, content or SEO role in the last 6 months
- Not cited in AI answers for the core category question a buyer would ask *(the GEO wedge — used as the why-now, never as a promise)*

**Lead Case Studies**

- **ShipCalm — 1,500%+ increase in monthly SQLs**, $30,000/mo SEO traffic value, monthly traffic 400 → 6,000+ *(CEO & Co-Founder: "I've worked with dozens of SEO companies in the past and can easily say that NextLeft is the best team out there")*
- **Midigator — 587% monthly increase in SEO leads** (B2B FinTech / chargebacks)
- **Widen** — 1,100% increase in organic traffic, 9,200 keywords, $64,400/mo traffic value *(quoted by their VP of Marketing)*

⚠ **Gap to close before launch:** ShipCalm and Midigator are percentage lifts on leads, not dollars. We need what those SQLs became — closed revenue, pipeline, or contract value. See Ask #1.

## ICP Segment 3
**Multi-Location & PE-Backed Home Services (5–40+ locations)**

Roll-ups and platform companies running plumbing, HVAC and electrical across many locations, where each location has its own Google presence, its own legacy site, and no one owning organic across the portfolio. This is the segment John named as NextLeft's actual special sauce — *"we're very good at multi-location... we work with private equity home services where they have like forty locations, 40 separate plumbers, electricians and HVACs — that's a whole special skill that a lot of people don't have"* — and in the same breath named the exact problem outbound solves: **"but trying to find those guys is really hard."**

**Decision Makers**

- CEO / President of the platform company
- VP Marketing / Director of Marketing at the platform or roll-up
- Operating Partner / Head of Value Creation at the PE sponsor
- Marketing Director at independent 5+ location operators

**Signal Points**

- PE or roll-up ownership — "acquired by", "platform investment", "portfolio company" via PitchBook, Crunchbase or press releases
- 5–40+ locations under one brand, or a multi-brand roll-up under one holdco
- Acquired 2+ locations in the last 12 months — integration is the moment website and SEO consolidation lands on someone's desk
- Fragmented Google Business Profiles across the footprint: unclaimed listings, inconsistent NAP, or separate legacy sites per location
- Ranking below a national competitor for "[service] [city]" across multiple markets in their footprint

**Lead Case Studies**

⚠ **This is the biggest gap in the whole plan.** NextLeft actively serves a 40-location PE-backed home services group and has published nothing about it. Their strongest differentiator has zero public proof. Closing this is Ask #1 and it likely promotes this segment above Segment 1.

## If the Current State Continues — What Goes Wrong

**The Best Closer Stays Part-Time**
NextLeft is running on 2–3 discovery calls a week. Earl — the strongest closer on the team — was moved from full-time to part-time purely because there aren't enough leads to justify the seat. That's a capacity decision made backwards: the constraint isn't closing ability, it's calendar volume. Nothing about the delivery side changes that. Only new pipeline does.

**The Proof Stays in Units That Don't Convert Cold**
NextLeft's public numbers are keywords and monthly traffic value. Those are the right metrics for a renewal conversation and the wrong ones for a cold open — a VP Marketing who's never heard of NextLeft doesn't convert "$613,000/mo in traffic value" into anything they can act on. Meanwhile the numbers that would land — Fiskars' 300% revenue lift, ShipCalm's 1,500% SQL increase — sit inside testimonial quotes instead of leading the pitch. The proof is strong. The framing is costing replies.

**The Highest-Value ICP Stays Unreachable**
The multi-location, PE-backed home services work is the thing NextLeft does better than almost anyone, and it's the one segment they can't source. Without an outbound motion pointed at it, the pipeline keeps filling from whoever happens to find them — which is why the last three closes came in at $1,500–$2,000/month single-location deals against a $5,600 average and a $4,000 MRR target. Inbound doesn't choose deal size. Outbound does.

**Deals Leak at Follow-Up, Unmeasured**
John raised this himself: *"a lot of these deals, especially when they're colder, you can have great conversations, you get a proposal, and then they don't close — and it's like, well, how often are we following up, how many times, what are we saying? Then measuring that."* Colder leads convert on follow-up discipline, and right now nobody owns or measures it. Adding volume on top of a leaky follow-up process wastes the volume.

**AI Search Moves Before They've Claimed the Ground**
NextLeft markets GEO hard — *"Win at AI search"* — with no published case study behind it. Their buyers are already asking the question: whether they show up in ChatGPT and AI Overviews, and how they'd even know. Every month that goes by, a competitor publishes the first credible AI-visibility proof point and owns the category conversation. Right now that's an open door.

## What We Need From NextLeft Before Launch

Two prerequisites. Neither is optional — the pilot guarantee is conditional on both.

**Ask 1 — Case studies with keyword-level specificity, and a dollar number on the B2B side**

John already said this is easy: *"we have already cataloged all our clients we ever had and have basically results and case studies for all of them, about 150 or so. It's all data, so I could format it like this right away."* What we need per case study:

- Before state and after state, with dates
- **The specific keywords won** — and critically, **who they outranked.** "Outranked Amazon for [category term]" or "beat [named national competitor] for [service] in [city]" is worth more in cold outreach than any traffic-value figure. Generic ranking claims don't produce replies.
- The revenue, lead or pipeline number where it exists — not the percentage alone
- What NextLeft actually did, in one plain sentence

Priority order for the pull:
1. **The PE-backed multi-location home services group (40 locations)** — currently zero public proof for their single biggest differentiator
2. **A dollar figure behind ShipCalm's 1,500% SQL lift and Midigator's 587% lead lift** — what did those leads become in closed revenue or pipeline?
3. Any case where they outranked a named national competitor or Amazon — one line each across the eCommerce set

**Ask 2 — Confirm the sales process behind the calls we book**

Outbound leads arrive colder than inbound. John already knows this and has run them before, so this isn't a lecture — it's a resourcing confirmation. We need to know before launch:

- Who owns the discovery call at volume — John, AJ, or Earl? Right now it's split across three people at 2–3 calls a week; at 8+ shows a month that needs an owner.
- **Is Earl coming back full-time when the calendar fills?** He's the strongest closer and he's the one currently sidelined by the lead shortage. If he returns on launch, this works. If not, we're booking into the same constraint.
- Who runs follow-up after the first call, and what does the cadence look like? This is the leak John named himself.
- Confirm the pilot's four qualified shows land on a calendar with real availability in week one — not three weeks out.

## Deal Math — worth raising on Thursday

John's own numbers: average client ~$5,600/mo, target of 2–4 new clients/month at ~$4,000 MRR, month-to-month with no lock-in.

At 25 qualified shows per quarter (~8/month) and a typical 15–25% cold-outbound close rate, that's roughly **1–2 closes/month** — landing under his 2–4 target on volume alone. Two things close that gap, and both are segment decisions rather than volume decisions: push ACV up by targeting multi-location and mid-market instead of single-location local, and get the case studies sharp enough to lift close rate. Worth naming honestly rather than letting him back into the math after month one.
